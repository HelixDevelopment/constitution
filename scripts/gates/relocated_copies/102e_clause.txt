Clauses (A)/(D) make root-cause investigation the first action on a spotted problem; §11.4.4 binds root cause, root fix and four-layer coverage for a defect found during a test cycle; §11.4.224(A) makes every executable work product test-first; §11.4.115, §11.4.135 and §11.4.146(D3) bind the permanent guard and the status custody of a fixed defect; §11.4.260(C) forbids a workaround presented as a fix. **What an issue is.** An ISSUE is any observed deviation between the expected and the actual behaviour, state or result of the product OR of the work around it. The definition decides; examples only illustrate it: a product defect; a test, gate, check or instrument that refuses wrongly, passes wrongly or measures wrongly (§11.4.201, §11.4.273); an agent that dies, stalls, loses work or reports what its evidence does not support; a tool, hook, wrapper or script that fails, hangs, races or exits with a misleading code; stale or mis-pointed state; a review or gate loop that cannot converge; host resource pressure or an unreachable remote. An intentionally induced failure observed exactly as designed — a deliberate RED baseline, the FAIL a §1.1 mutation induces, a golden-bad or negative-control fixture firing — is not an issue; it becomes one the moment it departs from its design (a RED that passes, a mutation that survives, a fixture that does not fire).

**Where an issue is tracked: two defaults, each with one exit.** (1) OWNED UNTIL PROVEN EXTERNAL. A cause is EXTERNAL — outside everything the project owns, such as a provider quota, a remote outage or a third-party component — only once captured evidence proves it (§11.4.201(4)); until then it is treated as owned. (2) A CHANGE'S OWN DEFECT. A change is its DIFF: the content it adds or alters, plus a throw-away script written only to produce it and discarded with it. Everything that existed before the change is already landed, even inside a file the change edits, and so is every KEPT tool the change uses (a committed script, generator, hook, gate, wrapper or service): a defect that such a tool writes into the diff lies in the tool, not in the diff. A change LANDS when it reaches a seam other actors consume: a merge into the trunk or into a branch other actors build from, a QA, release or production deployment, or a completion report. The actor's own unmerged branch, pushed or not, and the actor's own test target are not landing. An issue observed while an unlanded change is being made is CHANGE-LOCAL only when its symptom lies wholly in that change's own diff: a failure of the change's own new or altered code or tests, or a review finding on the diff. Every other issue observed during the change — in a kept tool, test, gate, script, hook, wrapper or service, in the process, the infrastructure or the agent orchestration, or with any part of its symptom outside the diff — is an (E.1) item at first observation, whatever its cause later proves to be; if (E.2) then proves that every factor lies in the diff, that item closes with its Type's §11.4.33 value, citing the change's test and review record. A change-local issue may be recorded first in the change's own durable record: a recorded RED test of its test-first loop (§11.4.224(A)) or its review record (§11.4.276, §11.4.235(D)). The DEFAULT: when (E.2) proves that EVERY factor of the cause lies in that diff, that record is the issue's tracking, and the change's own fix, tests and paired mutations are its root fix and guard. The DEADLINE and the EXITS: a change-local issue is proven wholly in the diff and fixed there before the change lands, or it is filed before the change lands; and the moment any of the following happens, each factor proven outside the diff (or, when none is, the issue itself) becomes a workable item under (E.1) at once, carrying the evidence from the change's record: (E.2) places any factor outside the diff; the change is about to land with the issue not both proven wholly in the diff and fixed there (a change found to have landed that way is the same trigger, acted on when found); or the change is ABANDONED. A change is abandoned when it is closed or discarded without landing, when its branch is deleted, or when it has had no recorded activity for longer than the staleness window the consumer declares (§11.4.35, §11.4.267(E)); the actor that closes, discards or deletes it files its open change-local issues first, and the §11.4.233 control-plane sweep files them for a change it finds stale. A consumer that has declared no staleness window has no change-local parking: every issue observed during its unlanded changes is an (E.1) item at first observation. Worked cases: a typo the actor introduces into its own edit of an existing gate, caught by that gate's test before landing, is the change's own; a kept generator that writes a wrong value into the diff is an issue in the generator, even when the actor corrects the output by hand; a new caller that passes empty input to a landed helper that mishandles it has two factors, and the helper's factor is an item even when the caller is changed to avoid it; a defect in an existing test, found while extending it, is in landed content. Clause (E) relieves only its own intake and guard duties here; §11.4.4 and §11.4.238 still apply where their own conditions hold.

**(E.1) TRACKED AT FIRST OBSERVATION.** Except for a change-local issue under default (2) above, an issue becomes a workable item through the §11.4.202 reporting path when it is first observed — or, when it recurs, is linked to its existing item (§11.4.214) — carrying what was observed, where, and the captured evidence. That includes a defect found in a kept tool, test, gate or script while doing other work, even when the change works around it or fixes it in the same commit (the fix then cites the item). One other record counts as tracking: a cause PROVEN external under default (1) and handled by a mechanism another anchor requires, within that anchor's declared bounds, is tracked by that mechanism's own record (agent registry, reap log, event stream) until the cause turns out to be owned or the anchor's declared escalation bound is passed; then a workable item is opened. An issue that is only mentioned in prose, a log or a session transcript has been LOST at intake (§11.4.197, §11.4.210).

**(E.2) ROOT CAUSE PROVEN WITH CAPTURED EVIDENCE BEFORE ANY FIX.** Clause (A)'s Iron Law applies unchanged to tooling, process, infrastructure and orchestration issues: the mechanism is reproduced, or otherwise proven from captured evidence (§11.4.199 exact sequence where a reproduction exists; §11.4.201(6)-(12) and §11.4.273 control-needled measurements), and the proven cause, every factor of it (§11.4.194(1)), is recorded with the issue. "Transient", "flaky", "probably a race" or "the hook is just slow" without that evidence is the §11.4.6 / §11.4.7 violation clause (A) already names.

**(E.3) THE ROOT CAUSE IS FIXED; NOTHING ELSE CLOSES THE ITEM.** Closure needs the change that removes the proven cause (§11.4.250 where workarounds have already stacked; §11.4.232 / §11.4.233 where the cause is orchestration or persistent-state mess), or a closure under (E.5). Clause (E) never forbids or delays a response another anchor requires or allows; it adds only conditions (ii)–(iv) below to such a response, as duties, never as a reason to withhold it. Any response that keeps work moving without removing the cause — whatever it is called — is permitted exactly when all four hold: (i) it is a mechanism a constitution anchor defines (a recovery that anchor requires or allows, or an escape it defines for a gate or seam), used where that anchor applies it, within its bounds and with its record; or, where no anchor defines one for the situation, it is a reversible action that no anchor forbids and that skips, overrides, exempts, weakens or removes no test, gate, guard or check; (ii) it is recorded with the issue — on its item, in the change's own record for a change-local issue under default (2), or in the mechanism's own record for a proven-external cause — and a recurrence links to that item instead of minting a new one (§11.4.214); (iii) a failed verdict stays the verdict of record: a crash of the artifact under test, or one not yet proven to lie outside it (§11.4.201(4)), is a failed verdict, and a re-run before the root cause is proven is a new observation, counted and surfaced (§11.4.264(F)), never a replacement verdict, with a suspected flake going to the §11.4.248(A) quarantine (re-runs that verify a proven fix are not responses of this kind); (iv) it never closes the item and never stands in for (E.2) or (E.4). Anything else is forbidden — for example an unrecorded `--no-verify`, a force-push (§11.4.113), a deleted, weakened or silently disabled check, a re-run until green, or an exemption no anchor defines.

**(E.4) A PERMANENT DETERMINISTIC GUARD THAT FAILS ON THE PRE-FIX ARTIFACT, FOR EVERY ISSUE WITH AN EXECUTABLE SURFACE.** The fix lands with a test that drives the real artifact through its real invocation path (§11.4.224(A), §11.4.276(D)), is observed RED against the pre-fix state with its precondition provenance recorded (§11.4.115(F)/(G)), turns GREEN on the fix in at least three identical runs (§11.4.50; ten for cycle-validation suites), joins the standing regression suite (§11.4.135), and is paired with a §1.1 mutation — canonically the fix's revert (§11.4.115(F)) — that the suite catches. The item is NOT closable without the root-cause record, the fix, the RED→GREEN verdict pair and the mutation (§11.4.146(D3); §11.4.226 evidence-class floor), except under (E.5).

**(E.5) CLOSURE WHERE (E.3)'s ROOT REMOVAL OR (E.4)'s TEST DOES NOT APPLY — clause (E) adds no terminal status and no closure route.** An item closes only in a terminal state another anchor already defines for its situation, with that anchor's own evidence: its Type's §11.4.33 value (`Bug` → `Fixed`, `Feature` → `Implemented`, `Task` → `Completed`), §11.4.90 `Obsolete` with one of that anchor's reasons, or a §11.4.112 structural-impossibility closure. An item that none of them fits STAYS OPEN and tracked — `Operator-blocked` where §11.4.21's conditions hold, otherwise open debt re-checked at every §11.4.90 release-gate audit — and is never forced closed. Applied to the cases that recur: (a) NO EXECUTABLE SURFACE — a prose, citation or documentation defect: (E.4) is not owed (§11.4.224(F)); it closes with its Type's value once the corrected text passes the §11.4.142 independent review and the §11.4.106 doc sync, shown to be caught by a decidable mechanical check of its defect class where one exists, the missing check otherwise recorded as an item. (b) A DEFECT IN A COMPONENT THE PROJECT DOES NOT OWN — a third-party tool, library or service; an own-organisation submodule is owned and is fixed at its root. The local mitigation and a real-path test of the project's detection and handling of the failure, GREEN under (E.4), form a linked `Task` that closes `Completed`. The defect item itself is not closed while the defect persists (§11.4.260(C)): it links an upstream report, or the recorded attempt and refusal, and stays open as upstream debt until the upstream fix is adopted with its own (E.4) guard or a §11.4.90 reason applies (for example the dependency is replaced); the mitigation is removed through a linked item when the fix lands. (c) AN EXTERNAL CONDITION WITH NO DEFECT, proven by (E.2): the item is re-typed `Task`, citing that evidence, and closes `Completed` once the project's handling is recorded and a real-path test of its detection and handling is GREEN under (E.4). In (b) and (c) the RED runs against the project before the handling was added, its precondition recorded as `observed` or `constructed` (§11.4.115(G)(1)); those `Task` items claim handling, never a fixed defect, so §11.4.115(G)(2) does not reach them. (d) NEITHER REPRODUCIBLE NOR SIMULABLE: §11.4.90 `Obsolete` with reason `not-reproducible` and that anchor's evidence; the guard it cannot have is a §11.4.3 SKIP-with-reason against a tracked item, and a recurrence reopens it (§11.4.214). A product-defect report closed `not-reproducible` because a project-local environment or working-tree condition produced it makes that condition an issue under this clause.
