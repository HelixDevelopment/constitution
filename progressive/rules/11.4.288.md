### §11.4.288 — All work on `main`, recursively: every current and future change in the project root and in every own-org submodule and constitution hub engine it uses is made on `main` and lands on `main` as soon as it holds its source-correctness GO (fetch, merge onto the latest `main`, fast-forward push to every upstream, never force); manual QA tests builds cut from `main` and gates the release tag, not the landing; product and flavor variants live on `main` as data packs; a branch, worktree or clone is a vehicle used only when strictly necessary, fast-forwarded into `main` and retired before the work is reported done; a submodule commit is followed in the same wave by the parent pointer bump on `main` (User mandate, 2026-10-26)

**Forensic anchor (operator mandate, 2026-10-26, `CRITICAL`), verbatim as typed:** "All work MUST BE merged into main and all change we do and will be doing also all done on main for main repo and all submodules we use fully recursively!" and "Make sure this our mandatory constraint and the part of our constitution Submodule and always followed and respected!"

**Operator decisions (2026-10-26, relayed by the conductor, not verbatim; they resolve the five questions D-1 to D-5 the first draft of this anchor left open).** (1) Work lands on `main` as soon as it holds its source-correctness GO; §11.4.185 manual QA tests builds cut from `main` and is a confirmation gate on releases and tags; this AMENDS §11.4.195(B) and §11.4.167(C). In the consuming project at the same moment, the root was on `main` and all 31 checked-out submodules were on a detached HEAD (the normal state of a submodule checkout, which this anchor does not forbid; see clause (D)). Work that lives only on a side branch is invisible to every clone of `main`, to every build cut from `main`, and to every agent that starts from `main`; it is the shape this anchor removes. Under decision (4) those refs are merged onto `main` and retired as a separate integration step; this anchor text does not perform or claim that merge.

**Terms (named once).** `main` is the canonical trunk of a repository (`main`, or `master` where that is the declared trunk; §11.4.113). An OWN REPOSITORY is the project root, every own-org submodule (organisation in the consumer's own-org list, §11.4.31 / §11.4.79(6)) at any depth, and every constitution hub engine (§11.4.28(C)). A THIRD-PARTY SUBMODULE is any submodule that is not an own repository (`third_party: true` in `helix-deps.yaml`). A VEHICLE is any branch, `git worktree`, scratch clone or copy-on-write project copy created to isolate a piece of work (subagent scratch clones, `isolation: "worktree"` agent runs, review sandboxes and §11.4.167(A) sibling copies included). INTERPRETATION (the author's reading, not an operator decision, UNCONFIRMED by the operator): the operator decisions name branches and worktrees; including scratch clones and copy-on-write copies is the author's extension, because such a copy holding a commit that is not on `main` is the same loss hazard; a scratch clone or copy that never holds a commit that is not on `main` (a read-only review sandbox, for example) is not a vehicle for the necessity and retirement duties of clause (B)(2) to (4). UNIQUE COMMITS of a ref are the commits reachable from it and not from the upstream `main` tip (`git rev-list --count origin/main..<ref>` greater than 0). SOURCE-CORRECTNESS GO is the independent review verdict of §11.4.235(A) / (D) on the §11.4.209 substrate, iterated to GO under §11.4.134 and bounded by §11.4.276.

**(A) Scope: all work, current and future, in every own repository, recursively.** Every change to an own repository (code, tests, gates, documents, configuration, governance text, generated artefacts that are tracked) is made on `main` and lands on `main`. This applies to the project root and to every own repository at every depth of the submodule tree. INTERPRETATION (UNCONFIRMED): the relayed mandate says "all submodules we use"; excluding third-party submodules from the commit-on-`main` duty is the author's reading, since we cannot land commits in a repository we do not own. A THIRD-PARTY SUBMODULE is consumed at a pinned ref and is never committed to by us (a needed change goes upstream to its owner or into a fork that becomes an own repository by recorded operator decision, §11.4.66); bumping its pin is a commit on the PARENT'S `main`. "Future" is literal: the rule binds every change made after this anchor lands, by any agent, subagent, track, script or human operating under this constitution.

**(B) Integration procedure and vehicles.** (1) DIRECT WORK ON `main` IS THE DEFAULT. A change lands on `main` the moment it holds its source-correctness GO; nothing else (no manual-QA wait, no approval wait, no open hardening item) holds a GO'd change off `main`, except the named quiescence waits of §11.4.84 (working-tree quiescence for commits) and §11.4.121 (no commit while a build writes tracked artifacts), which delay the landing only until their condition clears. Landing is the §11.4.113 procedure, unchanged: fetch every remote (§11.4.37 / §11.4.71), take the latest `main` tip across all upstreams as the base, integrate onto it with no `-s ours`, no rebase or reset that drops a commit, zero conflict markers and zero dropped files (§11.4.41 step 3), and push to EVERY upstream as a fast-forward (§2.1). Force-push of any kind stays forbidden (§11.4.113); no commit is lost (§9.2). (2) A VEHICLE IS PERMITTED ONLY WHEN STRICTLY NECESSARY: parallel agents that would otherwise write the same working tree (§11.4.20 / §11.4.70 / §11.4.84), a review or test sandbox that must not touch the live tree, or a destructive experiment kept away from `main`. Convenience, habit, or waiting for a later gate are not necessity. The vehicle's creator records which necessity applies. (3) A VEHICLE LANDS FAST-FORWARD-ONLY. The vehicle first merges the latest `main` into itself (merge, never rebase, the §11.4.188 mechanics), passes its source-correctness GO, and then `main` is advanced to it with `git merge --ff-only` and pushed fast-forward to every upstream. A vehicle whose landing would need a non-fast-forward step on `main` merges `main` into itself again first; `main` is never rewritten to make it fit. (4) RETIREMENT BEFORE DONE. Before the work item a vehicle carries is reported done (§11.4.15 / §11.4.33 closure, a §11.4.197 completion claim, a subagent hand-back, or a release), the vehicle's unique commits MUST be on `main` on every upstream (`git rev-list --count <upstream>/main..<ref>` equals 0 for every upstream), and only then is the vehicle retired: the branch deleted locally and on every upstream, the worktree removed, the scratch clone deleted (with `safe_rm_rf` or a `${VAR:?}`-guarded path, §11.4.287). Deleting a vehicle that still holds unique commits is forbidden (§9.2, §11.4.122). (5) There is no long-lived feature, release, product or flavor branch in an own repository; clause (C) states how the existing branch anchors read under this rule. (6) A subagent that works in a vehicle reports in its hand-back the vehicle, the necessity that justified it, its unique-commit count and whether it was landed and retired; a hand-back that leaves a vehicle with unique commits is a crash-class incomplete result under §11.4.147, never a completion. This hand-back duty is the author's mechanism for enforcing (B)(4) on subagents, not an operator decision.

**(D) Submodule commit, then parent pointer bump, in the same wave.** A commit made in an own submodule is pushed to every upstream of that submodule FIRST (fast-forward, on its `main`), and in the SAME wave the parent repository records the new gitlink in a commit on the parent's `main` and pushes it, recursively up to the project root. The parent push happens only after every upstream of the submodule reports the new commit (otherwise a fresh clone of the parent hits `not our ref`, the UNFETCHABLE class of §11.4.233(G)). A recorded own-org gitlink MUST be a commit reachable from that submodule's upstream `main` (equal to it or an ancestor of it); a gitlink to a commit that exists only on a vehicle is forbidden. A submodule checked out at a detached HEAD equal to such a commit is compliant: "on `main`" is a property of the recorded commit, not of the checkout's symbolic ref. Pointer drift, absence and unfetchability stay governed by §11.4.233(G); this clause adds that the recorded commit is on `main`. INTERPRETATION (the author's reading, not an operator decision, UNCONFIRMED): the same-wave parent pointer bump and the submodule-first push order implement the relayed requirement that each submodule's work be on `main` and recorded by its parent; the operator did not state the order, which follows from the unfetchable-pointer hazard described above.

**(E) Review and QA gates still apply and never keep work off `main`.** Every change still passes the independent review of §11.4.125 / §11.4.142 / §11.4.194 on the §11.4.209 substrate, iterated to GO (§11.4.134, bounded by §11.4.276), and the test and evidence rules of §11.4.4(b), §11.4.108 and §11.4.262. That review GO is the source-correctness GO a change needs before it lands on `main` (the reading of §11.4.142, "before acceptance/commit/build", is unchanged). Findings raised after landing land as FOLLOW-UP COMMITS ON `main`; they are never a reason to park a change on a side branch. §11.4.185 live manual QA tests builds cut from `main` and is the confirmation gate on releases and tags (operator decision (1)); it is never a reason to keep work off `main`. Background hardening of a landed change follows §11.4.230(D.4) / (E) and §11.4.235(A).

The rule is stated for git repositories; a consumer using another version-control system maps "`main`" to its declared trunk as DATA (§11.4.35).
