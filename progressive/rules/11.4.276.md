### §11.4.276 — Review-round budget: every independently-reviewed work item converges within a declared budget of at most 7 rounds (5–7, consumer-declared), by front-loading ground truth, class-exhaustive enumeration, real-path tests and a declared threat boundary, with a binding methodology change at round 3 (User mandate, 2026-10-05)

**Forensic anchor — verbatim operator mandate (2026-10-05, `CRITICAL`):** "Make sure we never ever get to such extremely high never ending review rounds count! Determine exact causes of issues we are facing with deep planning and exhaustive analisys so all work ALWAYS gets done with 5 to 7 reviews max! This MUST BE mandatory part of the constitution!!! Reasonable reviews rounds count with fully deterministically validated and verified working results produced!"

**Forensic FACTS (genericised; full citations in the consuming project's own continuation record).** One reference consuming project ran four independently-reviewed work items to 7, 10+, 30 and 39+ rounds in one week. Reading every round's recorded findings showed the count was driven by five recurring, avoidable mechanisms, not by an unusually hard problem: **(1) wrong-model ground truth** — a build-configuration fix and its OWN verifying gate were both built on a simplified mental model of an external build system's inheritance mechanism (values assumed to flow through a plain include chain, when the real tool clears the variable before a node's own include and merges ancestor values in afterwards); the gate even ran the real tool, but on a hand-simplified fixture, so it reported GREEN on a fix that could never work; it was caught only when a reviewer read the tool's own source and rebuilt the fixture on the tool's REAL composition path, after which the fix was rebuilt on the real path and landed in a single remediation round; **(2) instance-at-a-time closure of a known class** — a security gate was hardened against command-shadowing one command name per round (each of rounds 36, 37 and 38 found further unhardened names) after round 35 had explicitly considered and declined a mechanical whole-file enumeration because no decision-path classifier existed; in a second item a "close the whole class" instruction was met by fixing a syntactic proxy token rather than the class, and the same defect family returned in four consecutive rounds; **(3) fixer-authored tests that never drive the real code** — at rounds 25, 26, 27, 37 and again 38 a reviewer showed the newest regression test re-implemented, text-extracted, or never drove a verdict through the logic it claimed to guard, so the reviewer's own mutations of the real code survived the whole suite; **(4) non-binding escalation signals** — the heuristic-tower anchor (§11.4.250) was invoked about thirty times across these items (once, at round 5, with the explicit prediction "point fixes alone are likely to produce a Round 6 of the same kind") and the stall anchor (§11.4.267) had no declared retry bound anywhere in the project, so neither ever stopped the loop; a reviewer-recommended structural fix (re-executing the gate at its call seam in a scrubbed environment) was deferred in two successive rounds while in-file point hardening continued; **(5) scope drift** — a review loop absorbed an out-of-scope tangent (a separate, unrelated hardening effort surfaced mid-review) and the round counter kept accruing against the original item for the whole of that tangent, inflating its own count substantially before the tangent was explicitly separated out. The same record also contains genuinely new findings that legitimately needed another round (a shell-parser asymmetry that made a core test operator shadowable; a sentinel variable an attacker could pre-set) — iteration as such is not the defect. The defect is rounds spent re-discovering what one front-loaded investigation would have found, or accruing against an item the item never actually owned.

**What this is NOT (the §11.4.227 boundary, stated first).** §11.4.134 governs the review loop's GO bar (zero findings, zero warnings) and its obligation to keep re-running until that bar is met. §11.4.267 governs the shared attempt record, converge-on-evidence, and an undeclared stall bound `N`. §11.4.250 governs the heuristic-tower SIGNAL that a primitive is defective. None of the three bounds the review loop's LENGTH or binds a concrete action to a round NUMBER. §11.4.276 adds exactly that — clause (A) the ceiling, clause (E) the round-3 checkpoint and the ceiling escalation. Clauses (B)–(D) add no new substantive obligation beyond fixing WHEN existing duties must be discharged: (B) binds §11.4.6 / §11.4.99 / §11.4.102 phase 1 / §11.4.199 / §11.4.226 to a recorded artifact at a fixed point before the first fix; (C) binds §11.4.250 / §11.4.201(7)(b) to the round that first meets a nameable class; (D) binds §11.4.224(A) / §11.4.194(6)(d) / §11.4.115(F) / §11.4.235(D) to every remediation round's own regression set.

**The mandate (ALL hold):**

**(A) DECLARED ROUND BUDGET, HARD CEILING 7.** Every work item that passes through the §11.4.125 / §11.4.134 / §11.4.142 / §11.4.194 review loop carries a round budget `R_max`, declared by the consuming project as DATA (§11.4.35) in the closed range 5 ≤ `R_max` ≤ 7; an absent declaration resolves to 5 (the conservative value — the escalation in (E) fires earliest; §11.4.101). The ceiling is a constitutional literal because the operator supplied it verbatim (§11.4.246's operator-decided fleet minimum is the same pattern); the 5–7 range is the operator-mandated ceiling recorded with its own verbatim mandate above, not an invented `N` attributed to an external source (§11.4.267(E)) — the consumer's declared `R_max` within that ceiling IS its §11.4.267 `N` for the review loop, and remains consumer DATA. A round = one independent review verdict on the item, whether on uncommitted or already-pushed work, counted from the first review and recorded with the item's review record; a review that never received its brief, or crashed without a verdict, is recorded but not counted. Splitting, renaming, or re-filing the item does NOT reset the count unless (E)(3) applies. A tracked item created from a finding raised during a budgeted item's review inherits that item's consumed round count — the exception is a finding outside a scope/threat boundary declared before round 1 under (E)(2), which starts a fresh budget; item identity for recurrences follows §11.4.214 (link, never mint). *Amended 2026-10-30 by clause (G): `R_max` is 5 by default and the hard ceiling of a cycle; 6 or 7 only by a recorded operator decision naming the item; a follow-up item registered under (G.2) starts a fresh budget.*

**(B) GROUND TRUTH BEFORE THE FIRST FIX.** Before the first fix attempt — not after a reviewer finds the model wrong — for any change or finding whose correctness depends on the behaviour of a system the author does not own (build tool, compiler, interpreter or shell semantics, kernel, runtime, third-party library, protocol), the author MUST record a ground-truth probe: (1) the authoritative source of the behaviour (the tool's own source code or normative specification, cited by file and line — not a summary, not memory); (2) a minimal fixture executed on the REAL tool through its REAL composition path (the actual inheritance, sourcing, linking or dispatch mechanism the product uses — a hand-simplified approximation of that path does not count); (3) the recorded behavioural model the fix relies on. The fix's own verifying gate or test MUST exercise the same real path the probe established; a gate verified on a simplified model is wrong-layer evidence (§11.4.226) and cannot support a GO. Composes §11.4.6 / §11.4.99 / §11.4.102 phase 1 / §11.4.199 — it binds them to a recorded artifact at a fixed point in time.

**(C) CLASS-EXHAUSTIVE ENUMERATION ON FIRST INSTANCE.** When a finding is an instance of a class (bypass via name resolution, an unguarded external command, a masked character, a mutation-surviving test, a missed parse case), the round that fixes it MUST (1) name the class; (2) enumerate every member across the item's whole scope with a mechanical instrument whose null result is control-needle-proven (§11.4.201(7)(b)), and record that inventory; (3) close every member, or scope a member out explicitly with a reason and a tracked item (§11.4.197). Fixing only the reported instance when the class is nameable is a §11.4.276(C) violation. When the enumeration needs a tool that does not yet exist (for example a classifier separating decision-path calls from display-only calls), building that tool IS the remediation work — "no classifier yet" is not a reason to fall back to instance-by-instance fixes. A claim that a class is "closed" MUST cite the inventory artifact; a fix that closes a syntactic proxy instead of the class is an unclosed finding (§11.4.250).

**(D) REAL-PATH TESTS AND REVIEWER MUTATIONS ADOPTED VERBATIM.** Every regression test added in a remediation round MUST drive the real shipped artifact through its real invocation path (§11.4.224(A)) — a test that re-implements the guarded logic inline, or runs extracted source text outside the artifact's own process, guards nothing and is a `source-defect` finding under §11.4.235(D). Every mutation a reviewer reported as surviving is added to the item's mutation set verbatim and must FAIL the suite before the next review is requested (composes §11.4.194(6)(d) / §11.4.115(F)). The fixer runs the item's full regression set and the reviewer's previous attacks against the fix before requesting review; the dispatch seam REFUSES to start a review when that record is absent (never counted as a round because it is never dispatched). A review that WAS actually dispatched and returned a verdict always counts as a round, regardless of whether its triggering record was later found deficient — the record requirement gates DISPATCH, it is never grounds to retroactively erase a verdict that already issued.

**(E) BINDING METHODOLOGY CHANGE AT ROUND 3, AND THE CEILING ESCALATION.** (1) At round 3, and at every later round, the round's fixer MUST first record a convergence assessment classifying every finding so far as new-class / same-class-as-earlier / regression-introduced-by-a-fix / wrong-layer-evidence. If two or more findings share a defect class named under (C) (not merely a shared §11.4.235(D) finding layer) with at least one non-MINOR finding in that shared class, any finding reappeared after being reported fixed, or any finding was wrong-layer evidence, the next round MUST be a structural round: a fresh fixer agent instance that did not author rounds 1–2 (the reviewer remains structurally separate throughout per §11.4.240), re-deriving (B) from first principles, re-running the (C) enumeration over the whole scope, and replacing the approach rather than adding a further layer (§11.4.250). A structural fix recommended by a reviewer may not be deferred past this point without a recorded operator decision (§11.4.66). (2) Every review declares, before round 1, its scope and acceptance boundary (and, for adversarial/security reviews, the threat model — which actor capabilities are in scope, and the seam at which the defence lives) so that a zero-finding GO under §11.4.134 is reachable; findings outside the declared boundary are tracked items, not loop-extending findings. The boundary is fixed at round 1 and changes only by recorded operator decision — narrowing it mid-loop to reach GO is a §11.4.6 fabrication. (3) If round `R_max` returns anything but GO, the item MUST NOT run round `R_max`+1 under the same approach. It stops and goes to a recorded operator decision (§11.4.66) carrying: the finding classification, the evidence of which of (B)–(D) was skipped or failed, and the options (re-scope into smaller slices per §11.4.148; replace the approach with the structural design as a new item; or accept residual LOW findings only through a §11.4.271 waiver). Only a recorded operator decision opens a new budget. The escalated item is parked per §11.4.101 / §11.4.267(F) — it parks only that work unit; the autonomous loop (§11.4.126) continues all other work, and the pending decision is surfaced in every §11.4.97 progress report until resolved. Every breach is also recorded as a process escape and audited for which of (B)–(D) would have prevented it (the §11.4.238(C) pattern applied to the review loop).

**(F) NOTHING HERE LOWERS THE BAR.** The budget bounds the NUMBER of rounds; it never lowers what a round must establish. §11.4.134's zero-finding, zero-warning GO bar is unchanged; its re-run obligation is bounded PER APPROACH by this anchor's `R_max`, after which (E)(3) governs rather than a further same-approach round. §11.4.209's review substrate, §11.4.235(D)'s finding-layer classification and §11.4.262's machine-created evidence are likewise unchanged. Reaching round `R_max` without GO never produces a GO, a "good enough", or a silent close: the item stays open until a recorded operator decision under (E)(3).

**(G) DEFAULT AND HARD CEILING 5 ROUNDS, AN EXTENSION ONLY WHEN ABSOLUTELY NEEDED, AND MINOR FINDINGS RIDE THE NEXT ITERATION (amendment, operator mandate 2026-10-30, `CRITICAL`).** Verbatim operator mandate (2026-10-30 11:27 CET, `BACKGROUND :: CRITICAL`), quoted exactly as typed, typos preserved [sic]; its first two sentences are implemented by this clause, the rest by §11.4.230(F): "Make sure we do not have more than 5 rounds unless absolutely needed! Minor issues can stay but to be tackled in next iterations. We MUST aim to make as much as possible releases consicutivelly! Even after we do release something we continue testing, fixing and polishing in the background and preparation iof next better version that is goinf to be released! This is continuous process wehich MUST BE already defined and explained to details in our constitution Submodule! Add any details we may be missing there so this is absolutely clear!" This is the operator-mandated ceiling recorded with its own verbatim mandate in its own anchor, the one form of `N`-bound literal §11.4.267(E) admits: the literal is the 5 of the first sentence. The mandate states no length for the "unless absolutely needed" exception, so no extension length is a constitutional literal (G.3). PRECEDENCE: this clause amends clause (A) (the consumer-declared 5–7 range and the inheritance of a spun-out item's round count), clause (E)(3) (an operator decision as the only way past `R_max`) and clause (F) (no GO short of zero findings at the ceiling); where they differ, (G) governs. Clauses (B)–(E)(2) are unchanged.

**(G.1) BUDGET.** `R_max` is 5 by default and is the hard ceiling of a review cycle. An `R_max` of 6 or 7 is allowed only by a recorded operator decision (§11.4.66) naming the item; a consumer-wide declaration of 6 or 7 no longer suffices (pending operator confirmation of removing the consumer-wide right; until then the conservative reading above applies).

**(G.2) MINOR-CLASS FINDINGS NEVER EXTEND THE LOOP.** SEVERITY (a NEW rule of this clause; §11.4.235(D) governs only `finding_layer`): every finding a review records carries, beside its §11.4.235(D) `finding_layer`, exactly one `severity` from the closed set {`critical` | `important` | `minor` | `nit`}: `critical` — blocks the batch (the artifact is wrong, unsafe or unproven where it matters, or a proof-of-batch gate or test is broken); `important` — a real defect or gap that is not blocking; `minor` — a small correctness, clarity or robustness gap with no effect on any excluded surface below; `nit` — style or wording. The severity is recorded by the REVIEWER and is never lowered by the producer, who may contest it only through a fresh review round (the same producer-verifier rule §11.4.235(D) applies to `finding_layer`, §11.4.240 / §11.4.249). A finding with no recorded severity, or one that cannot be decidably classified, is not minor-class (§11.4.201(4)). A finding is MINOR-CLASS when the reviewer records it with `finding_layer` `test-instrumentation` or `process-doc` (§11.4.235(D)), or with `finding_layer` `source-defect` and severity `minor` or `nit`, AND it touches none of: a §11.4.239 critical-invariant surface, a security finding (§11.4.184), data safety (§9), host safety (§12), or the evidence that proves this batch (which §11.4.235(D) already classes as a `source-defect`). Letting a `minor` or `nit` `source-defect` ship as a follow-up is a dated relaxation of §11.4.134 and §11.4.235(D), recorded in place in both (pending operator confirmation; the conservative reading of the mandate's "Minor issues can stay"). When every open finding of a round is minor-class, the review loop ends: the reviewer records the verdict `GO-with-follow-ups` naming each finding, and each finding becomes a tracked item in the consumer's FOLLOW-UP REGISTER — the §11.4.93 workable-items store, linked to its parent item, never a separate store — assigned to the next iteration (the next increment, or the next candidate under §11.4.230(F)). The register location is consumer DATA (§11.4.35): a consumer that keeps a dedicated follow-up register (for example a `docs/FOLLOW_UPS_NEXT_PHASE.md` generated from the store) records it there as a VIEW of the §11.4.93 store, never as a second source of truth; absent a declaration, the §11.4.93 store alone. The parent item then lands under §11.4.288. A follow-up item starts a fresh round budget, because it is a new iteration. `GO-with-follow-ups` is never reported as a zero-finding GO (§11.4.6), and §11.4.134's zero-finding bar binds each follow-up item in its own loop.

**(G.3) AN EXTENSION ONLY WHEN ABSOLUTELY NEEDED, ITS LENGTH NEVER A CONSTITUTIONAL LITERAL.** The length `E_ext` of an automatic extension cycle is consumer DATA set by a recorded operator decision (§11.4.66, §11.4.35); its default is 0, which means no automatic extension (pending operator decision on any non-zero default). If round `R_max` ends with an open finding that is not minor-class and the consumer has declared `E_ext` > 0, ONE extension cycle of at most `E_ext` further rounds runs without a per-item decision if, and only if, the open set contains (a) a `source-defect` of severity `critical`, or (b) a broken proof-of-batch stack — a build that does not produce its artifact, or a proof-of-batch gate or test (the §11.4.235(D) evidence that proves this batch's fixes) that cannot run, or cannot fail on the broken artifact (§11.4.201); a gate or test that guards only future regressions is a `test-instrumentation` finding and never triggers (b). An `important` `source-defect` does not trigger an automatic extension (pending operator decision): the item goes to (G.4), where the recorded operator decision may route that finding to a follow-up in the next iteration. The trigger is recorded with the finding ids before the first extension round, and that round is a structural round under (E)(1). An extension cycle is never opened for minor-class findings (they go to (G.2)), and never more than once per item: the absolute ceiling is `R_max` + `E_ext` rounds. With `E_ext` = 0 every open non-minor finding at round `R_max` goes straight to (G.4).

**(G.4) AT THE CEILING.** If round `R_max` ends with an open non-minor finding and no extension runs under (G.3), or the extension cycle ends without GO or `GO-with-follow-ups`, clause (E)(3) applies unchanged; the recorded operator decision may grant a per-item extension of a stated length, re-scope, or route a finding to a follow-up in the next iteration: the item stops, goes to a recorded operator decision, and only that work unit is parked.

**(G.5) WHAT (G) DOES NOT CHANGE.** §11.4.134's re-run obligation within the budget, clauses (B)–(D), the §11.4.209 substrate, the §11.4.235(D) layer classification, §11.4.262 machine evidence, and every release seam (§11.4.40, §11.4.185, §11.4.236). A `GO-with-follow-ups` is a source-correctness GO for §11.4.235(A) only because no open finding is above minor-class; that is the dated relaxation recorded in place in §11.4.134, §11.4.235(D) and the §11.4.288 Terms. Honest boundary (§11.4.6): (G) moves minor work to the next iteration and caps the loop; it does not make a deferred minor finding harmless, so every follow-up stays tracked and visible until closed.

**(G) gates.** The contract of `CM-REVIEW-ROUND-BUDGET` (OWED-GATE-111) is amended: `R_max` 5 unless a recorded operator decision names the item for 6 or 7; rounds `R_max`+1 to `R_max`+`E_ext` are allowed only with a declared `E_ext` > 0 and a recorded (G.3) trigger; any round past that, or a second extension cycle, never without an operator decision. New recommended gates: `CM-REVIEW-EXTENSION-CYCLE-JUSTIFIED` (FAIL: an extension round with no recorded trigger or with `E_ext` undeclared or 0, a trigger citing only minor-class or `important` findings, a trigger citing a gate that guards only future regressions, a second extension cycle; golden-FALSE: with `E_ext` > 0 declared, a trigger citing a `critical` `source-defect`) + `CM-REVIEW-FOLLOW-UPS-REGISTERED` (FAIL: a `GO-with-follow-ups` naming a finding that is not a tracked item linked to the parent; a minor-class label on a finding in an excluded surface of (G.2); a finding with no recorded severity, or a severity lowered by the producer, counted as minor-class; a `GO-with-follow-ups` reported as a zero-finding GO; golden-FALSE: every follow-up present, linked and assigned) + paired §1.1 mutations (open an extension cycle for a nit; drop one follow-up item; label a security finding minor — each MUST make its gate FAIL). Gate code is a separate work item, NOT claimed shipped (§11.4.6 / §11.4.227); registered as tracked deferrals `OWED-GATE-172` and `OWED-GATE-173`. No escape hatch — no `--extension-for-minor`, `--second-extension-cycle`, `--drop-follow-up`, `--minor-means-done` flag.

**Honest boundary (§11.4.6).** This anchor does not promise that every item converges within `R_max` rounds. Some problems are hard, and a determined adversarial reviewer can keep finding real defects in an in-process defence whose threat boundary is wider than its design. What the anchor removes is the class of round inflation the forensic record shows to be avoidable: rounds spent discovering a wrong external-system model, one member of an already-known class, a test that never drove the real code, a stall signal nobody was required to act on, or an unrelated tangent silently accruing against the wrong item. When a problem is genuinely hard, (E)(3) turns an open-ended loop into a bounded, evidence-backed operator decision. The cap counts rounds, not effort; it does not license a shallower review to stay under it. The operator's own wording ("ALWAYS gets done with 5 to 7 reviews max") is read here as a ceiling with a mandatory escalation at that ceiling, not as a guarantee every item physically closes within it — this reading is recorded explicitly and was surfaced to the operator at landing per §11.4.66.

**Classification: universal (§11.4.17)** — no hardware, vendor or project literal; the consuming project supplies `R_max` (5–7), its review-record location, and its probe and inventory artifact paths as DATA per §11.4.35.
